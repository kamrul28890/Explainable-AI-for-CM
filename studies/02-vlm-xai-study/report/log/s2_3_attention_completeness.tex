\chapter{Stage 9 Completeness, Redefined on Attention (F7)}
\label{chap:s2-3}

\section{Goal}

Stage~9 (bounded completeness) asks whether an explanation was offered and whether it held up. As designed,
``held up'' meant the answer flips when Stage~4's token ablation removes the named region. Finding F4 showed
that ablation fails its own negative control --- a 51.7\% artefact floor against a 5.9\% signal --- so the
designed metric correctly computed a quantity with no discriminative power, and was marked not reportable
(F7). This phase replaces the broken half.

\section{What Was Done}

The decision (2026-11-10) was to redefine ``held up'' on attention, which Stage~5 already measures without
any intervention and which survived its own null (F6). \texttt{09\_completeness.py -{}-basis attention}
classifies each row:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{no usable explanation} --- no region was named;
\item \textbf{supported} --- the share of attention mass inside the named region exceeds the share of the
image the region covers (lift $>1$);
\item \textbf{weak} --- a region was named but attention inside it is at or below its area share.
\end{itemize}

It writes \texttt{completeness\_attention\_\{model\}.csv}. The ablation basis stays the default so the frozen
file reproduces, and its numbers are never reported. The report heads the section ``attentional, not
causal'' and prints the share of regions at a lift of 2$\times$ or more beside the headline.

\section{Standard vs.\ Non-Standard Choices}

\textbf{Non-standard, and it must be argued in the paper.} The claim changes from causal (``removing the
region changes the answer'') to attentional (``the model looked where it said''). That is a weaker claim,
and the paper states it as one. ``Supported'' is crossed by any lift above 1.0, which is why the 2$\times$
line is reported beside it: under half the named regions draw twice their area's share of attention.

\section{Implications}

Completeness is reportable again, on a basis that has passed a null test. A metric defined over another
metric inherits that metric's validity; this one now inherits Stage~5's, which was checked, rather than
Stage~4's, which failed.
