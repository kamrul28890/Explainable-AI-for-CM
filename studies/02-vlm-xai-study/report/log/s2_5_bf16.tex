\chapter{The Unquantised Arm: Is 4-bit Compression the Cause?}
\label{chap:s2-5}

\section{Goal}

Every Study~2 result used Qwen2.5-VL-7B compressed to 4 bits (nf4) to fit the 8~GB card. A reviewer can ask
whether the poor explanation results are a property of the model or of the compression. The A40 has room
for the unquantised model in bfloat16, so this phase runs the full battery on it.

\section{What Was Done}

A new tier, \texttt{qwen7b-bf16}, loads the same checkpoint folder at the same pinned revision
(\texttt{cc594898\ldots}) with quantisation off. The chains take the model as a parameter; a non-primary tier
gets its own state directory and model-suffixed output files in the same A40 tree, so the two tiers compare
without machine noise. Every stage ran: 0--10, plus 8 and the attention basis of 9.

Compression changes \emph{what} the model answers --- most of all on rule\_4 (a worker in the danger zone of heavy machinery), where the
unquantised model finds three times as many annotated violations, with intervals that do not overlap. It
barely changes \emph{how good the explanations are}: every explanation metric moves by two points or less.
The unquantised model is also less steady under sampling, especially on its NO verdicts.

\section{Problems and Solutions}

\textbf{Stage 6 stopped on its parse-rate guard.} After 100 rows only 90\% had all five sampled replies
readable, below the pre-registered 95\% floor. The first hypothesis --- the 128-token reply cap cutting the
reply off before the final ANSWER line --- was wrong: a 200-call diagnostic found the longest reply was
94 tokens. Instead, at temperature 0.7 the model sometimes replies with the single word ``NONE'' (11 of 13
failures) or ``ANSWER: MAYBE''. Per reply that is 2.6\%; but one bad reply in five failed the whole row.

\textbf{Decision (2026-11-10): grade the guard per reply, as Stage~2's is.} The guard exists to catch a
degrading prompt, which a per-reply rate still does. An unreadable reply still counts as a disagreement in
the agreement rate, so no 4-bit figure changed. The report gives the unreadable-reply rate on its own line.
bf16 Stage~6 was rerun from scratch; the tripped attempt is kept in \texttt{results/scholar-a40/superseded/}.
This is a change to a pre-registered guard after it tripped, and the paper states it as such.

\textbf{One Stage 8 row short.} bf16 has 1,599 rows, not 1,600: the shared seeded sampler rounds each rule's
proportional share, and for bf16's NO verdicts the shares round to 799. The sampler is shared with Stages
4 and 6 and their files are frozen, so it is left as is and noted. One further blur reply out of 7,995 was
unreadable.

\section{Standard vs.\ Non-Standard Choices}

\textbf{Standard.} An unquantised ablation of a quantised model.

\textbf{Non-standard.} Running it through the identical battery on the same GPU, so the comparison isolates
compression from machine, data and code.

\section{Implications}

The poor explanation quality reported for Study~2 is not a compression artefact: the unquantised model
explains no better. The paper can say so with a measured comparison. Accuracy is a different matter ---
rule\_4's sensitivity under 4-bit is a compression effect and should be reported as one.
