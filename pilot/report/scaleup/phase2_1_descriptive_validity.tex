\chapter{Descriptive-Accuracy Validity: Graded Score, Monotonicity, Mask Mode, and a Text Probe (Phase 2.1)}
\label{chap:p2-1}

\section{Goal}

Phase~1 made the masking metric test the right region and report a worker-loss-corrected number. Phase~2
asks a different question of each metric: does it measure what it claims, or an artifact the tabular
XAI methods it was borrowed from never faced? For descriptive accuracy the plan (item 4.1) names four
validity fixes: add the missing \emph{text}-ablation half (not just visual masking); expose a
\emph{graded} continuous signal so effects register as magnitude, not only a boolean flip; add a
\emph{monotonic-masking invariant} that flags the fallback-interaction signature; and check that the
flip rate is not an artifact of the \emph{black mask} itself by re-running under other mask modes.

\section{What Was Done}

\subsection{A continuous graded score}

The proxy's compliance decision is a threshold on a continuous quantity, but the pilot only ever kept
the boolean. A pure helper, \texttt{fraction\_covered}, returns the graded coverage in $[0,1]$, and the
answer logic was refactored into two pure verdict functions (\texttt{\_presence\_verdict},
\texttt{\_rule4\_verdict}) that return \texttt{(answer, graded\_score)} together. \texttt{graded\_score}
is now carried on every \texttt{AnswerResult} (fraction of workers with a nearby safety object for
presence rules; safe fraction for rule\_4; NaN when no worker exists to score). The refactor preserves
the frozen boolean answer exactly --- compliant iff the graded fraction is $1.0$ --- which the results
confirm below. The descriptive metric gained \texttt{graded\_score\_drop} (baseline minus masked), so a
mask that reduces coverage without flipping the answer still registers.

\subsection{Monotonic-masking invariant and mask modes}

A \texttt{non\_monotonic} flag marks any sample where masking top-1 flipped the answer but also masking
top-2 un-flipped it --- removing evidence should only ever preserve or add flips, so this isolates the
fallback interaction. And \texttt{mask\_region} gained an \texttt{"inpaint"} mode (OpenCV Telea), the
least out-of-distribution mask, alongside black and blur, so the flip rate can be measured against the
masking style itself.

\subsection{A text-ablation probe, honestly labelled}

True text-token ablation --- drop the concept word from a question and re-ask --- is only well-defined
for a native-VQA model. Florence-2 answers by \emph{grounding} a phrase, so there is no question to
delete a token from. Per an explicit decision on this project, the closest faithful analog is
implemented now and reported \textbf{separately, with the limitation stated everywhere}: \emph{swap the
rule's specific object phrase for a concept-free placeholder} (``object'') while reusing the baseline
worker detection (the image is unchanged), then re-decide. This measures whether the \emph{specific
object word}, rather than any detectable object, drives the compliance answer.
\texttt{answer\_rule\_text\_ablated} implements it; every report labels it a proxy approximation, not a
native-VQA ablation, and its numbers are never merged with the visual-ablation numbers. A native-VQA
text ablation remains a Phase~5 deliverable on Qwen2.5-VL.

Fifteen unit tests were added test-first (graded fraction, both verdict helpers, graded-score
recompute, graded drop, non-monotonic flag, inpaint mode).

The \texttt{non\_monotonic} flag counts 15 samples under area ranking --- exactly the 15 the pilot's
Chapter~5 found by hand --- and 22 under rule\_aware ranking, matching Phase~1.1's finding that the
worker-loss interaction relocates to the top-2 stage once the worker is no longer top-1. The invariant
now captures automatically what previously took manual tracing.

\subsection{The graded score is right --- and currently binary, for a documentable reason}

The graded score behaves correctly but, on this pilot, adds no signal beyond the boolean --- and
\emph{why} is the finding. Florence-2 detects at most one worker per image: of 163 samples, 151 have
exactly one worker box and 12 have none; \textbf{zero have more than one}. With a single worker, ``the
fraction of workers covered'' can only be $0$ or $1$: the baseline graded score is $1.0$ in 103 samples
and $0.0$ in 48, with \textbf{none strictly between}. So a graded drop occurs exactly when the boolean
answer flips (coverage $1\!\rightarrow\!0$), and there are \emph{zero} samples with a partial graded
drop but no flip.

This is not a defect in the metric --- it is the metric doing its job as a diagnostic. The graded score
is implemented and correct; its power-recovery benefit is gated on \emph{multi-worker detection}, which
Florence-2's current single-box grounding does not provide. This connects directly to decision D4 (dense
region captioning / exhaustive enumeration) and the Phase~5 detectors: the moment a scene yields two or
more workers, the graded score begins measuring partial compliance the boolean cannot. The instrument
is in place ahead of the detector that will exercise it.

\subsection{Text ablation adds independent signal}

Under rule\_aware ranking, the proxy text-ablation flips the answer in 38.0\% of samples, against a
38.7\% visual-ablation top-1 flip rate --- nearly the same rate. But they are \emph{not} the same
samples: of the flips, 30 flip on text ablation but not visual, and 31 flip on visual but not text ---
roughly half-overlapping.

That the two ablations flip at similar rates on \emph{different} samples is the substantive result: the
answer's dependence on the concept \emph{word} is real and largely distinct from its dependence on the
visual \emph{region}. This is exactly the ``missing multimodal half'' the metric was faulted for
lacking --- recovered here in the only form the grounding proxy admits, and quarantined from the
native-VQA numbers so the two are never conflated.

\section{A Problem This Surfaced, and Its Resolution}

The graded score's binary behavior is itself the surfaced problem: descriptive accuracy cannot measure
\emph{partial} explanation sufficiency while the detector returns a single worker, so the metric is
coarser than its definition allows. The resolution is not to patch the metric --- it is already correct
--- but to feed it a detector that finds every worker. That is decision D4 and the Phase~5 detection
work; Phase~2.1 deliberately puts the graded machinery in place first, so the improvement is a data
change, not a code change, when the detector arrives.

\section{Standard vs. Non-Standard Choices}

\textbf{Standard.} Reporting a graded alongside a binary metric, checking robustness to the masking
operator, and separating text from visual attribution are ordinary multimodal-XAI hygiene.

\textbf{Non-standard, and the discipline.} Two choices. The text-ablation is implemented as the closest
honest analog a grounding proxy allows and is labelled as such \emph{everywhere}, with its numbers kept
strictly separate from the native-VQA ablation to come --- rather than either faking a VQA number or
dropping the measurement. And the graded score is shipped \emph{before} the detector that makes it
pay off, with its current binary behavior reported plainly as a diagnostic of the single-worker
limitation rather than hidden --- so the metric's readiness and its current ceiling are both on the
record.

\section{Implications}

Descriptive accuracy now reports a graded signal (ready for multi-worker scenes), a mask-mode-robust
flip rate, an automatic non-monotonicity flag, and a separately-quarantined proxy text-ablation that
demonstrably adds independent signal. Two threads feed forward: the graded score is a standing argument
for D4 / better detection, and the proxy text-ablation is the grounding-model baseline the Phase~5
native-VQA text ablation will be compared against.
